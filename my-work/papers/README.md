## Practical Challenges in Reserve Risk

Kevin Chan and Michael Ramyar (October 2016)

- File: [2016-chan-ramyar-practical-challenges-in-reserve-risk.pdf](2016-chan-ramyar-practical-challenges-in-reserve-risk.pdf)
- Source: [IFoA website](https://www.actuaries.org.uk/system/files/field/document/Plenary%203%20-%20Practical%20Challenges%20in%20Reserve%20Risk_0.pdf)
- Keywords: reserving, reserve risk, stochastic reserving, bootstrap, Mack's model

**Summary.** A practical guide for general insurance actuaries to the problems met when quantifying reserve risk, with suggested solutions. It opens with a short tutorial on reserve risk (process risk, parameter risk, MSEP; analytical and bootstrap estimation). The core argues that the "scaling issue", forcing the reserve distribution's mean to equal a separately set best estimate, comes from inconsistencies in data, model and parameters between the reserving and reserve risk processes, and is a form of model specification error. The authors' long-run remedy is to make stochastic reserving part of the reserving process itself, so best estimate and distribution come from one model. The paper then addresses ten common misconceptions: for example, that the bootstrap is at fault, that industry-triangle CoVs are usable benchmarks without removing diversification, and that small CoV movements are meaningful. A simulation study on Mack's model (Appendix A) shows how far estimated CoV can sit from the true CoV, and suggests that about 14 or more years of history give an estimate within 10% of the truth more than half the time. Other topics: netting down a gross aggregate distribution (a "conditional ceded" approach), sensitivity to assumptions, and the one-year horizon.

The paper permits free distribution in its full original form; this copy is unaltered.
